# Accessibility

We want to make games people can play regardless of disability, language or
the device they own. Accessibility belongs in the design from the beginning,
and that's what we're working toward.

This statement covers [severedsignal.com](https://severedsignal.com) and our
video games. It says what we aim for and how to tell us when something gets in
your way.

Last updated 13 November 2026.

## How we work

- We build accessibility into every feature as we make it, and check each
  change against our accessibility rules before it goes out.
- We check our games against the
  [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/) and
  against [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), using the W3C's
  [guidance on applying WCAG to software](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/).
- We aim for WCAG 2.2 Level AA on the website.
- When something falls short, we track it until it's fixed.
- We aim to include disabled players in every playtest.
- We subtitle every spoken line and show who is speaking.
- We caption every video we publish.
- We run our games and trailers through a flash-checking tool before they go
  out, and we never call a game safe for photosensitive epilepsy.
- In our documents, headings never skip a level, each link says where it goes,
  and every image has a text description.

## Unaccounted

*Unaccounted* is in development for Windows, macOS and Linux, in English. This
section says what we aim for in the finished game. Some of these features
aren't built yet.

### Controls

- You can remap almost every action, with two keyboard or mouse bindings and
  one controller binding for each. Escape always pauses.
- The game binds each key by where it sits on the keyboard, not by its letter,
  so the movement keys are in the same place on any layout. Prompts name each
  key as your own keyboard labels it. On a French keyboard, for example, you
  move forward with the key labeled Z, and the prompt says Z.
- Crouch, Sprint and Scan can each be held or toggled.
- You can play with a keyboard and mouse, a keyboard alone, a mouse alone, a
  controller alone or a touchscreen. Control Scheme, under Controls, picks
  keyboard and mouse, keyboard alone or mouse alone, and Touch Controls adds
  on-screen controls.
- With a mouse alone, nothing has to be held down. You click to walk and to use
  things, and turn by dragging or by resting the pointer at the edge of the
  screen. Page Buttons, under Accessibility, lets you page through long lists
  with a button.
- On a controller, button prompts match Xbox, PlayStation or Nintendo Switch
  pads, Stick Response and Stick Deadzone tune the sticks, and vibration can be
  turned down to nothing.
- The keys that look around have their own speed setting.
- You can set the game to ignore accidental repeated presses, and you can play
  with switches.
- You answer the census by picking from lists, so you never have to type.
- The menus use your system's pointer, so a size or color you've set there
  still applies.

### Seeing

- Text Size, under Accessibility, offers Normal, Large and Largest, and the
  menus' boxes and art can grow with the text too.
- You can switch the menus to a plain font that's easy to read.
- Gamma and Contrast, under Display, adjust the whole screen, menus included.
- High Contrast, under Display or with the H key during play, grays out the
  room. Doors and other things you can use keep their colors, and they gain an
  outline while Object Highlights is on.
- Colorblind Mode, under Accessibility, applies a correction filter for
  protanopia, deuteranopia or tritanopia.
- The game never uses color alone to tell you something. Focus shows as a line
  or ring, things you can use have an outline, and the census marks each answer
  with a symbol and a word.
- Object Highlights outline what you can use, in a color you choose. While
  they're on, Scan outlines everything you can use at once. Prompts name the
  action and its key.
- You can change the size and color of the aiming dot.
- The retro look draws the room in large pixels with a fine dither pattern over
  it. Under Display, turn off Retro Filter to remove the pattern, and turn off
  Pixelated World to draw the room at your screen's resolution.

### Hearing

- Sound Captions say what each sound is and show an arrow pointing to where it
  comes from, with a note when it's on another floor. Turn them on under
  Accessibility and choose where they appear. They follow Text Size.
- Music, voices, sound effects and ambience have separate volume controls, and
  you can switch to mono sound.
- You can find every clue without sound.

### Motion and comfort

- The camera doesn't bob, shake or sway, there's no motion blur or depth of
  field, and the game never moves the camera for you.
- The game has no flashing or strobing lights.
- Field of View, Mouse Sensitivity and Stick Sensitivity are under Controls,
  along with settings to invert looking up and down and to make the look keys
  turn in steps instead of smoothly.
- You can turn off decorative motion, such as smoke, steam and the animations
  in the menus.
- The [health warnings](https://severedsignal.com/health) list the settings
  that help with motion sickness, patterns and long sessions.

### Time and saving

- Play has no time limits. The in-game clock moves only when you act, never on
  its own, so you can stop and think as long as you like.
- You can pause at any time. You can save in ten slots or with a quick save,
  and three autosaves sit alongside them without ever replacing your own saves.
- The game autosaves every five minutes of play by default. Under Gameplay, you
  can change that to every two or ten minutes, or turn it off.

### Screen readers and narration

- On Windows, macOS and Linux, the menus give screen readers the name of each
  control and announce prompts and save messages.
- The game can also read its menus aloud itself, without a screen reader.

### Help and difficulty

- You choose how hard the game is when you start a new game.
- The game teaches you how to play as you go, and each setting says what it
  does.
- You can check the day's task and reread what you've found.

## Website

[severedsignal.com](https://severedsignal.com) has no settings of its own. It
uses your light or dark theme, your increased-contrast setting or contrast
theme, and your browser's zoom and text size. You can use every page with a
keyboard alone. Nothing on the site plays by itself, and the only animation is
the thin reading-progress bar along the top, which follows your scrolling. The
site runs no JavaScript and sets no cookies. We aim for it to work in any
current browser on a computer, tablet or phone, with a screen reader or
magnifier, and at any zoom up to 400 percent.

## Report a barrier

If something makes one of our games or our website hard or impossible for you
to use, please tell us, even if you're not sure it counts as a bug.

- Fill in the
  [accessibility form](https://github.com/Severed-Signal-Studios/.github/issues/new?template=2-accessibility.yml)
  on GitHub. For a website barrier, name the page and your browser.
- Email [support@severedsignal.com](mailto:support@severedsignal.com). You
  don't need a GitHub account.
- Tell us on [Discord](https://discord.gg/nz9bkGb2Fv).

Tell us where the barrier happens and what makes it hard. If you like, tell us
how much it gets in your way, how you play, any assistive technology you use,
which settings you've changed and what would help. You don't have to tell us
about a disability, and you don't need screenshots or recordings. You can write
in English or Swedish.

Issues on GitHub are public, and other members of our Discord server can read
what you post there, so don't share medical details or anything else you'd
rather keep private in either place. Only we see what you email us. If you
report by email or on Discord, we add the barrier to the issues for you without
your name or personal details, and send you the issue's link so you can follow
it.

## What happens next

We read every report ourselves and answer as soon as we can, but we're a small
studio and can't promise a response time. We track each barrier you report in
our public
[list of accessibility issues](https://github.com/Severed-Signal-Studios/.github/issues?q=is%3Aissue+label%3Aaccessibility).

We work first on barriers that stop someone from playing a game or using our
website, then on those that make it hard, and then on small annoyances. If we
can't fix a barrier yet, we say so in the issue and share any way around it we
know. When a barrier comes from a game engine, an operating system, a browser
or a store, we make sure it's reported to the people behind that product too.
When that report is public, we link it from our issue. When a fix is out, we
say so in the issue and invite you to try it.
